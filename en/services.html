Guide

IT infrastructure in Mozambique: what changes when it is done properly

Most Mozambican companies only think about their technology infrastructure when something stops working: the network slows to a crawl in the middle of month-end, a camera failed to record the one night it mattered, or the telephone system stops routing calls on the busiest day of the week. The pattern repeats across very different sectors — construction, hospitality, banking, industry, retail and the public sector — and the cause is almost always the same: the infrastructure was assembled in pieces, at different times, by different people, with nobody documenting the whole.

This page gathers what we have learned over more than fifteen years installing and maintaining IT infrastructure in Mozambique, organised by area. Each section links to the relevant service page, where you will find the technical detail, the frequently asked questions and the geographic coverage.

It always starts with the cabling

There is no such thing as a fast network over poor cabling. It is the least visible part of the investment and therefore the first to be cut from the budget — usually with consequences that only surface two years later, when nobody can work out which cable goes where. A structured cabling system solves that with three simple things: every point labelled, every point tested, and a certification report handed to the client when the work is finished.

The practical difference shows up on the day something fails. In a certified, labelled installation, finding the fault is a matter of minutes. In an installation with no standard, it is an entire afternoon of a technician unplugging cables to discover which is which — and that afternoon costs more than the labelling that was skipped. It is also worth sizing for growth: pulling ten spare points during the works is cheap; opening walls six months later to accommodate new hires is not.

Electronic security is not just cameras

A security system that works combines three layers that support one another. CCTV cameras record and deter, but they only cover what was framed correctly — which is why the site survey, before any equipment is purchased, has more influence on the outcome than the equipment itself. Intruder alarms warn in real time, provided the sensors are placed so they do not raise false alarms: a system that goes off for no reason ends up switched off by the staff themselves, and from that point it protects nothing. Access control closes the loop, answering the question recordings rarely answer on their own: who came in, through which door and at what time.

There is also an administrative benefit that usually pays for a good part of the investment. A biometric access control system turns attendance recording into an automatic report, exportable straight into payroll, instead of hand-signed sheets nobody can audit.

Connectivity: where fibre solves what copper cannot

Copper network cable has a practical limit of about ninety metres and is sensitive to electrical interference and lightning strikes. Whenever you need to link separate buildings, cross a yard or cover a long warehouse, fibre optic is the technically correct answer — and it is safer too, because it carries no electricity between structures. What separates good work from bad here is certification: without OTDR testing and a measurement report, there is no way to prove the installed run is within loss limits.

Above the physical layer sits configuration. Many companies buy more bandwidth to fix slowness that has nothing to do with the provider, and everything to do with saturated switches, badly sized Wi-Fi or the absence of segmentation. In telecommunications and networking we run the diagnosis first and show where the congestion is; only then is equipment worth discussing. Where the operation cannot stop, we configure redundancy across two providers with automatic switching, so the changeover happens without anyone intervening.

Communication: voice and content over the same network

Once the network is solid, it starts carrying services that used to need their own cabling. Voice solutions with IP PBX and VOIP cut the cost per extension, let staff answer the company number away from the office, and give management something analogue lines never did: reports on answered calls, missed calls and average waiting time. For hotels, schools and institutions, IPTV solutions distribute television, in-house channels and digital signage over the existing data network without duplicating infrastructure — provided that network was sized for it, which is precisely where most IPTV projects go wrong.

Support is what separates a supplier from a partner

Installation is a moment; operation is permanent. What distinguishes useful technical support from a vague promise is having response times in writing, with different priorities for a broken printer and for a server that is down. Two things are worth checking in any contract: whether preventive maintenance is scheduled, and whether backups are tested by restoring them rather than simply configured. A backup that has never been restored is not a backup — it is an assumption.

Online presence: the other half of the investment

Keeping the infrastructure running holds the operation up, but it does not bring new customers. That is why we also work on website design that is fast on mobile, SEO optimisation and Google Business Profile, social media management with messages actually answered in good time, and custom software development for when no available program follows the company’s process. Across all of these the measure of success is the same: enquiries generated and time saved — not the number of posts or of features.

What it costs, how long it takes and where we work

None of those questions has an honest answer before the site visit. The cost of a cabling project depends on the number of points and the state of the building; the cost of a CCTV system depends on the area to cover and the recording days to retain. What we can commit to beforehand is the method: free site visit and diagnosis in Maputo and Pemba, a proposal with technical design and timeline within three working days, and travel to other provinces quoted separately and transparently. We work across the whole country, for companies, government institutions, industry and homes.

If you are not sure where to start, start with the assessment. We come to the site, look at what is already there and tell you frankly what is worth keeping, what should be reconfigured and what is only causing problems — even when the answer is that you do not need to hire us right now.
